# Last Light — economy v0.2 (implemented baseline)

This version supersedes [v0.1](economy.md). It incorporates the critical-oxygen window, exact turn sequence, feasible action schedule, inspectable NPC policy, hard procurement limits, and complete medicine rules. **The baseline is implemented in the JS game and NEAR testnet contract. Automated parity and integration tests pass; human enjoyment and balance remain unproven.**

See [the current build](game-build.md) and [committed verification evidence](evidence/README.md). The retrospective discussion below preserves earlier design reasoning; it is not a current implementation checklist.

## Core match

Twenty **decision windows** of 15 seconds each, two humans + one rule-based NPC. Settlement delay may make a match last longer than five wall-clock minutes; a new 15-second decision window begins only after the previous round is settled. Everyone starts with 25 credits and a vehicle with an empty two-fuel tank. The colony treasury starts with 180 credits; no credits are created mid-match. Online inventories, credits, queued orders and settlement are authoritative in the deployed NEAR testnet contract. The browser must never call a pending move complete. Travel graph: depot↔habitat and depot↔clinic only (no direct habitat↔clinic route); each leg takes one merchant action and one tank fuel. Plant deliveries happen at the habitat.

Depot prices: oxygen 2, medicine 2, fuel 1. The depot is an external finite supply source; its payments enter a sink, not the treasury. The depot initially has three oxygen and releases three more after each of rounds 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15 (18 total); four medicine are available for the match; fuel is finite (provisional total 24). Each merchant can hold at most three oxygen. Cargo volume 6 (oxygen 2, medicine/fuel 1), tank fuel separate, maximum tank 2.

The habitat starts with 4 oxygen and consumes 1 at the end of every round. The clinic starts with 1 medicine and 2 health; deadlines at rounds 6, 12, 18 consume one medicine. Missing a deadline reduces health by 1; health 0 collapses the colony. Clinic storage maximum 2. Clinic accepts only up to 3 merchant medicine in the match, and only while storage <2. Later delivery can prevent a second miss but does not restore health. Clinic medicine payout: 5 if storage=1; 8 if storage=0 (regardless of countdown). Oxygen payout per unit, repriced as stock increases: stock >=4 → 4; stock 3 → 5; stock 2 → 6; stock 1 → 7; stock 0 → 8. Habitat storage maximum 7. Oxygen delivery is accepted only within an 18-unit *oxygen-equivalent procurement quota*: each purchased oxygen consumes 1 quota, each plant fuel delivery consumes 2. Plant fuel costs the treasury 4 per unit and may consume at most three fuel units over the entire match. **When its sale resolves**, each accepted fuel is paid for, consumes two quota units and immediately creates two oxygen, subject to the habitat storage cap. A later merchant in the same round therefore sees the updated stock and price. The plant sale is rejected atomically if it would overfill storage, exceed quota or fail its minimum payout; it never reserves future space. For oxygen and medicine, the accepted quantity is `min(requested quantity, merchant cargo, remaining district storage, remaining procurement quota/cap)`. Reprice accepted units sequentially, then require a **positive** accepted quantity, enough treasury balance, and the merchant's minimum **total** payout; otherwise reject the whole action without moving goods or money. Unaccepted cargo stays with the merchant. **Plant fuel is all-or-nothing**: its requested quantity must fit remaining three-fuel lifetime cap, storage, shared quota and treasury at resolution; no deferred or partial production. Previews must display the maximum fillable quantity and estimated total, not imply all requested units will sell.

Oxygen at zero **after consumption** puts the colony in CRITICAL state; it survives until the **next** consumption. A delivery or plant production must make stock positive by then. A failed second consumption at zero collapses the match. Thus an 8-credit payout is reachable during a last-chance rescue. The baseline treasury is pre-funded to cover **every legal purchase**: conservative maximum payout 168, starting balance 180. Thus treasury bankruptcy is **not a baseline strategic threat**; it is an invariant to test, and only lower-budget variants can make it a crisis. At the end of round 20, habitat needs >=2 oxygen and clinic needs >=1 medicine and health >=1. A shared collapse gives no winner. On survival, highest end credits + cargo salvage − initial credits wins. Salvage is integer credits: `oxygen units + medicine units + floor(unsold saleable fuel units / 2)`; vehicle tank fuel has zero salvage. Listed goods stay owned by the seller and count once after offers are cancelled/unlocked at match end. Scores, balances and ownership freeze at terminal settlement; ties share placement in the prototype.

## One exact round sequence

0. **Round opens:** each player, including NPC, sees current authoritative state and public depot/medicine schedule. Orders carry `match_id` and `round_id` and may be submitted or replaced until that round's onchain cutoff. Included orders are public; neither the UI nor the rules promise secrecy. Reacting to visible included orders is allowed, but network visibility/inclusion latency may vary and must be tested. Waiting is legal.
1. **Orders close:** only valid orders included before the specified round's cutoff enter its snapshot. A transaction included **after** that cutoff is rejected as stale, with no state change and no carry-over to the next round. Each player has **one** of: depot load/refuel, travel one leg, sell a specified good into a district, or wait. Depot loading may combine goods and tank refill in one action; buying, movement and selling remain separate. Travel does **not sell on arrival**. Return-fuel checks block impossible trips.
2. **Resolve:** execute each available action sequentially. Priority rotates **inside each three-round block and the block's starting seat rotates too**. For round `r` (starting at 1), first-seat index is `((r−1)//3 + (r−1)%3) % 3` over `[A, B, NPC]`, then the other two follow cyclically. First priority on oxygen-shipment rounds **1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16** is therefore **A, B, NPC, A, B, NPC**, not A six times. NPC has no special queue. Each accepted oxygen or plant-fuel sale updates inventory, quota, treasury and payout immediately; a later order sees the new state. A missing order means wait; an invalid purchase or sale rejects **only that merchant's action**, and resolution continues to demand. Priority resolves outcomes; it does not make publicly submitted orders secret.
3. **Demand:** habitat consumes 1 oxygen if available; clinic consumes 1 medicine on deadline rounds if available, otherwise loses 1 health.
4. **Check:** if clinic health reaches 0, or habitat was already CRITICAL and still could not consume oxygen, collapse. If habitat has 0 oxygen after this round, mark it CRITICAL for the next round. On round 20 require habitat >=2 oxygen and clinic >=1 medicine and health >=1. Otherwise settle surviving merchants' scores.
5. **Next round opens** with accepted, rejected and late-order receipts shown. Never render an estimate as a completed trade.

The *round* is the authoritative execution unit: among valid included orders, priority decides contested goods, not transaction ordering. Inclusion before the cutoff still matters; unequal network latency and public-order reaction remain fairness risks. Settlement must be **idempotent**: duplicate settle calls cannot repeat payouts, production, medicine demand or oxygen consumption. No merchant submission means wait; an invalid action rejects locally and cannot abort the round. The next decision window opens only after the previous round settles, so real elapsed match time can exceed five minutes. Once the match ends, all new economic actions reject; pending offers are cancelled/unlocked and final balances and scores become immutable. The testnet implementation and authorization/cutoff/idempotence verification are documented in near-integration.md and docs/evidence/.

### Pre-confirmation preview (required product behavior)

Before a player commits, show current location, arrival round, earliest possible sale round, travel fuel and return requirement, remaining cargo volume, visible depot stock, current quoted per-unit payout and likely range after competing orders, chosen minimum payout, treasury/quota remaining, habitat oxygen runway, clinic's next deadline, and whether the next missed consumption is terminal. Label estimates as estimates, already included rival orders as **visible**, and future/unincluded actions as unknown. The confirmation state must distinguish queued, included, settled, and rejected. The FAQ and HTML concept site are updated alongside this specification; check `python scripts/build_faq.py --check` after editing the source FAQ.

## Feasibility witness, not balance proof

An **executable scripted witness**, `python scripts/check_witness.py`, checks depot releases, one action per merchant per round, cash, tank/cargo limits, separate travel and sale, rotating priority, district stock, medicine deadlines, quota and treasury against one schedule. Oxygen sales of 3 units occur on rounds **3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18**. A buys/refuels at 1, travels 2, sells 3, returns 4; B buys 4, travels 5, sells 6, returns 7; NPC buys 7, travels 8, sells 9, returns 10. Later A buys 10, travels 11, sells 12; B buys 13, travels 14, sells 15; NPC buys 16, travels 17, sells 18. A delivers medicine on rounds 7 and 16; B on 10, using gap rounds for buying, travelling, selling and returning. The witness ends with oxygen **2**, clinic medicine **1**, clinic health **2**, and treasury **72**; no stock goes negative. These are **one scripted schedule's outputs**, not strategy simulations, player behavior, or onchain settlement. The witness now runs through the shared deterministic engine, which also has regressions for isolated rejection, idempotent settlement, partial oxygen fills, medicine failure, and the plant lifetime cap. It does not model stale network inclusion or version-bound resale acceptance; add those before calling the rules implementation-ready. The optional resale market remains unimplemented.

A single merchant making only oxygen runs could sell at earliest on rounds 3, 7, 11, 15 and 19—at most **15 oxygen**, versus **18 extra oxygen** needed for the final reserve. That does **not** prove the NPC cannot sustain oxygen alone, because a fuel-plant run could substitute for two oxygen per fuel. Nor does it prove two humans will have meaningful choices; compare NPC-only, NPC-plus-one-human, and no-NPC scenarios under complete medicine requirements.

## Why the critic was right

- The previous rule made the 8-credit oxygen state unreachable; the CRITICAL round makes it reachable and visible.
- The previous oxygen arithmetic proved only resource totals, not action feasibility. The witness above attempts both, but it is still not a game simulation.
- A rescue's **per-trip** margin does not prove it beats waiting. Waiting for stock 1 improves a 3-unit trip from 12 revenue/4 net (at stock >=4) to 18 revenue/10 net, with 3 oxygen costing 6 and two fuel costing 2. Compare whole-match strategies across many conditions, not individual trips. Stock 0 in the CRITICAL round pays 8+7+6 = 21 revenue/13 net if the order settles before demand. This depends on travel and rival execution.
- The implemented NPC policy is deterministic and readable in game/rules.js and the Rust model: sell carried goods at their district, return to the depot, account for public oxygen reservations, buy oxygen when supply is low, then medicine when the clinic is empty. Full parity and sandbox tests cover this policy. Human tests comparing behavior with and without it remain useful; optional resale behavior is not implemented.
- The earlier treasury 180 calculation is only valid with enforced caps. The shared quota (oxygen delivered + 2 × plant fuel <=18), at most 3 medicine purchases and <=3 plant-fuel purchases imply a loose maximum treasury payout of 180; because plant fuel replaces oxygen quota, a tighter ceiling under the proposed per-unit prices is **168**, reached with 18 oxygen at 8 and 3 medicine at 8. Storage limits also prevent excessive early oxygen dumps. No loan or unpaid accepted obligations. The actual per-unit repricing means this ceiling is intentionally conservative.
- Medicine storage, deadlines, payout and failure are specified above. Its capped capacity forces restocking after deadlines rather than prepaying the entire match.

## Quota sensitivity: 18 is a baseline, not a sacred number

Initial oxygen 4 + purchased/produced oxygen-equivalent quota 18 − 20 rounds of consumption = **2**, exactly the required finish. A successful match must use the quota fully; plant conversion changes its *source* but cannot add surplus beyond the quota. That may become a checklist. Compare quota **18** versus **20** under identical seeds and opponents. Conservative maximum payouts with three medicine purchases are **168** and **184** respectively (`quota × 8 + 3 × 8`). The baseline treasury of 180 safely covers the first ceiling, **not** the second. For quota-20 tests, use a treasury of at least 184; for an isolated quota effect, test *both* quotas with the same larger treasury as well. Compare survival, profitable choices, late-game slack and whole-match winner diversity. Do not silently raise quota without revisiting budget and depot supply.

## Evolving economy: a claim to demonstrate, not just describe

The schedule supplies **starting conditions**, not scripted outcomes. Successful merchant actions can change habitat scarcity, colony quotes, ownership, merchant cash, medicine availability, and plant output. Optional depot offers add **player-set resale prices** and a distinct seller-versus-rescuer decision; until implemented and used by players, that is an *experiment*, not a demonstrated feature. The NPC must react using the same state and rules, not trigger predetermined scene changes. A match should evolve under fixed code and rules, without a developer adjusting prices between rounds.

For a judge-visible counterfactual, replay the **same starting balances, shipment timetable, deadline schedule, round priority and NPC policy** twice: (A) early oxygen delivery; (B) withholding plus an accepted depot offer, if that optional market survives tests. Record actual per-round stock, quote, owner, payout, treasury and winner/collapse. Then show where the two traces first diverge because of player choice. If the same economic strategy always wins, or if the depot offer never clears, do not call this an evolving player-run market. No random disaster is needed to prove divergence, but currently neither path has been run as a complete competitive match.

## Optional player-run market experiment (not locked for build)

The current colony/depot loop gives players ownership of goods but not price-setting between merchants. Trial a **single depot-only offer per merchant**: a seller with cargo at the depot spends one round action to list an exact whole lot and chosen price per unit; goods are locked, remain owned by seller and count toward cargo capacity. A buyer physically at the depot spends one round action to accept a **previously included** offer by exact ID, version, lot quantity and per-unit price. On execution, contract atomically checks that the same version/terms are still active, seller stock, buyer credits and **all cargo types** against volume, plus the depot's shared finite fuel inventory if the buyer refills; any mismatch rejects without a transfer. Replacing an offer increments its version and invalidates stale acceptances. The buyer may refill their tank from the same finite depot fuel inventory in that accept action, paying the depot for fuel, not minting it. A seller leaving the depot cancels the listing atomically; explicit cancel or replacement takes an action. No self-buy, partial fill, matching engine, off-depot trade, volume rewards, or same-round acceptance of an offer that did not exist at round open. The NPC may list and accept under the same one-action, cash, information and priority constraints, with a disclosed maximum resale price based on its expected colony payout after fuel/time costs.

The first unassisted rescue through resale is tight: round 1 buy, 2 list, 3 accept/refuel, 4 travel, 5 sell. With no other delivery, round 5 is the last rescue opportunity. Require an action-level witness for both this rescue and competing-order rejection; the existing isolated transfer check does not prove either.

This is **experimental**, not a final feature. It strengthens player price-setting and potential track fit only if players actually choose to trade and those trades create interesting decisions. Compare matches with and without the market, including unsold listings, emergency buyouts, NPC offers, and rejection/priority races. Avoid claiming the first prototype has player-run trade until a working example exists.

## Open adversarial tests

- Does intentionally hoarding to the last CRITICAL round beat repeated early trips in *total profit*? Compare when a rival or NPC pre-empts, when shipment schedules vary, and when the treasury has less headroom.
- Does the NPC subsidize selfish humans by rescuing too reliably, or steal human opportunities? Run identical seeded scenarios with and without the NPC and with different priorities.
- Can a trailing player recover, or does mutually assured collapse become their strongest move? Test both hoarding, both cooperation, and one saboteur.
- Is medicine genuinely strategic, or just a dull timer? Can the plant reduce future oxygen profit in a way players notice?
- Can a merchant manipulate quotes through repeated orders, benefit from settling first disproportionately, or exploit cutoff latency? Rotating priority must be implemented, not described only.
- Do mobile players voluntarily rematch? A functional economy is not automatically a fun game.
