Get rich supplying a colony that might not survive your next deal.

A twenty-round strategy game for two human merchants and one rule-based rival. Each decision window lasts 15 seconds; settlement may extend the match beyond five minutes. Buy finite supplies. Choose which district to save. If the colony collapses, everyone loses—even the richest player.

01 / The premise

Every customer is part of your life-support system.

The same sale can save a district, earn you money, and keep your rival in the match. Profit and survival are not separate modes; they are the same decision.

01 / STOCK

Buy the shipment.

Depot supplies arrive on a public schedule, but your rivals can reach them first. Your cargo hold limits what you can carry.

02 / ROUTE

Spend fuel and time.

Oxygen keeps the habitat breathing. Medicine meets clinic deadlines. Fuel moves you—or powers emergency oxygen production.

03 / DECIDE

Sell or hold.

A desperate district pays more. Waiting might improve your margin, cost you a rival's trade, or kill the only market you have.

THE DILEMMA

“I can save the colony now. But if I wait, I might win.”

02 / Exact sequence

No invisible move. No phantom sale.

One merchant action per 15-second decision window. Settlement can extend the round; the next window starts after it settles. Buying, travelling and selling are distinct actions. Arrival does not automatically sell.

  1. Orders open. Everyone, including the NPC, sees public state and submits or changes one action. Included plaintext orders are visible, not secret.
  2. Orders close. Orders name match_id and round_id. Late inclusion rejects; nothing rolls forward.
  3. Actions resolve. Buy/refuel, move, sell or wait. Priority rotates within three-round blocks and also shifts each block: shipment rounds 1/4/7/10/13/16 begin A/B/NPC/A/B/NPC. A failed action rejects locally; duplicate settlement must not repeat consumption. Plant fuel changes stock immediately.
  4. No separate production phase. Plant fuel creates oxygen when its sale resolves; later merchants face the new stock and quote.
  5. Colony consumes. One oxygen each round; medicine on published clinic deadlines. Health and oxygen are checked.
  6. Next state opens. Receipts and actual ownership appear. Zero oxygen starts one visible CRITICAL rescue round.
Design constraints

Finite means finite.

The treasury begins with 180 credits and cannot mint more; capped baseline purchases cost at most 168, so treasury exhaustion is not a baseline crisis. Depot supply is finite. The baseline colony accepts at most 18 oxygen-equivalent units, up to three clinic medicine and up to three plant fuel. Each plant fuel replaces two payable oxygen units. Quota 20 is a test variant, not a settled balance choice.

Oxygen and medicine may fill partially up to storage/quota/capacity, only if the total payout meets the merchant’s minimum; plant fuel is all-or-nothing with a cumulative three-fuel match cap. A quote is an estimate; a completed trade is a fact.

Execution truth

Orders close. Included plaintext choices are public. Priority rotates among valid orders; settlement or rejection changes ownership, never a preview.

Show four distinct states: queued, included, settled, rejected. A rival or earlier plant-fuel sale may change the final price. Every order names a match and round; stale orders reject, never roll over. A player sets a minimum total payout; a partial fill can settle only if it meets that minimum. Invalid actions cannot stop colony demand.

04 / One mechanic

The longer you wait, the more it pays. Until it doesn't.

Oxygen payout depends on current habitat stock. A three-unit delivery reprices each unit; the emergency price is real only in the one-round CRITICAL rescue window.

Not a promise

Waiting from stock four to stock one improves a single trip's margin. It may still lose the match: a rival can sell first, the depot can run dry, or the colony can collapse.

This calculator assumes storage for three units, remaining quota and treasury funds, and two travel fuel; actual partial fills may pay less or reject against the minimum total. It is a rules preview, not a transaction simulator.

06 / Evolving economy

Same start. Different decisions. Different market.

The shipment timetable is fixed. What merchants do with the goods should change stock, prices, ownership, profits and whether the colony survives. That outcome is a test target, not a finished gameplay claim.

Path A / Deliver early

Supply improves.

Oxygen reaches the habitat. Its shortage payout falls. Merchants may pursue medicine, fuel conversion, or the next shipment instead of waiting on an emergency premium.

Path B / Hold or resell

Scarcity compounds.

A merchant withholds cargo or offers it at a chosen price at the depot. A rival or NPC may buy and attempt the rescue; the extra action and price can change who profits—or cause collapse.

Demo target: replay identical starting balances, shipments, deadlines, priority and NPC policy twice. Show actual round-by-round differences from player choice. No random disaster or developer price patch needed. Depot resale remains an optional experiment until tested.

07 / Optional experiment

What if another merchant needs your cargo more?

Depot-only player offers could add a second price-setting decision. This is a proposed test, not a committed game feature.

LEAVING DEPOTAutomatically cancels offer

Sell to a rival—or save the colony yourself.

Example: buy oxygen for 2 credits, offer it at 4. A rival may buy it and attempt the colony delivery. Accepting an offer takes an action and only works after it was included in a previous round. Acceptance binds exact offer ID, version, quantity and price; replacing an offer invalidates a stale acceptance. Any refill draws from the same finite depot fuel, and buyer capacity counts oxygen, medicine and saleable fuel. Nothing is automatically matched.

The earliest unassisted resale rescue needs five actions (buy, list, accept/refuel, travel, sell) and reaches the round-5 last chance. We keep it only if an action-level rescue and rejection witness works and players use it in full matches. It must not make rescues impossible through added waiting, or become a checklist feature built just to fit a track.

09 / What can break it

A good pitch is not a good match.

These are the tests that decide whether Last Light is worth building further.

Waiting may dominate.

A near-collapse delivery earns more per trip. We need to measure whole-match results with travel, rival actions and scarce depot shipments—not praise the rescue margin in isolation.

The NPC may do everyone's work.

A survival-first merchant could become a free rescue service. Run identical seeded scenarios with and without it, and keep its rule inspectable.

Latency may feel unfair.

If the UI says a player owns the last shipment and the settlement rejects it, the game loses trust. Shipment priority must rotate by block as well as by round; honest pending states are required, not just aesthetic polish.

Players may choose collapse.

A merchant who cannot win could withhold essential cargo. No proposed rule proves this is solved. Test it with actual people before adding progression incentives.
